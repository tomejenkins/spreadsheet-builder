Why department-level planning matters

A site can have enough total hours and still miss volume because labor is in the wrong department or lacks the required skills. This separate illustrative example shows the decision sequence we will build next.

Planned engine: Calculate demand and available hours for every department → identify qualified surplus associates → allocate eligible transfers while preserving donor minimum coverage → compare remaining overtime capacity and cost → recommend hiring by department and lead time. This is a fixed teaching scenario, not yet linked to the single-department calculator above.

Critical constraints: associate skill matrix, shift overlap, minimum department staffing, travel/setup time, transfer efficiency, overtime caps, and hiring ramp-up. Without those, a site-wide sum can be misleading.

How the model works

Demand: Forecast units ÷ productivity = required productive hours.

Supply: Associates × paid hours × (1 − absence rate) × productive share.

Overtime: Additional paid hours × attendance factor × productive share. OT is limited per available associate.

Attrition: Existing headcount declines weekly at the specified rate; fractional headcount is used for expected-value planning.

Hiring: The recommended cohort covers the largest modeled post-lead-time shortfall in base capacity, assuming hires reach full productivity immediately. Onboarding, cross-training, transfers, shift coverage, hiring cost, and budget constraints are not yet modeled.
